How it works

  1. 1

    Deploy

    After clicking, on Runpod pick the RTX PRO 4500 (or another card with 32 GB or more) and click Add volume (180 GB, already sized for you) — that's where your models and films live.

  2. 2

    Create your password

    Click Connect → HTTP Service and create your password on the first screen. For safety, a new studio can only be claimed in the first 15 minutes; after that, restart the pod to reopen setup. Prefer to set it up front? Create a Runpod Secret and set STUDIO_PASSWORD to {{ RUNPOD_SECRET_studio_password }} under Set overrides. A plain value works too, but Runpod shows environment variables in plain text to anything with access to your account.

  3. 3

    Start creating

    First boot downloads ~128 GB of models in the background. The studio opens in about a minute; paste a free Hugging Face token when it asks, and everything is ready in about 10 minutes, depending on the machine's network.

New to Runpod? The step-by-step quickstart walks you through it, and What you need lists everything to have ready.

FAQ

Which GPU should I pick?

The RTX PRO 4500 (32 GB, Secure Cloud). It's what the whole workflow is tested on, including previs films and the 4K export. If it's not available, pick another card with 32 GB or more, like an RTX 5090. A 24 GB card like the RTX 4090 runs images and video, but 4K export may not fit.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything — models, projects, generated media — lives on your pod's own persistent volume. Nothing is sent to a third-party API unless you optionally enable an LLM key for script breakdown.

What happens if I stop or terminate the pod?

Stopping the pod keeps the volume intact (you keep paying for storage, not compute). Terminating the pod deletes the volume — and everything on it — permanently.

Can my characters talk?

Yes. Give a character a voice in Cast: describe it (“gravelly, tired man in his 60s, slow drawl”) or upload a short clip of someone talking. Every line that character speaks in your storyboard is recorded in that same voice, so they sound like one person across the whole film, and the export mixes the lines in. Voices run on your pod with Qwen3-TTS (Apache-2.0).

Why does video need a Hugging Face token?

LTX-2.5, the default video engine, and its reference-sheet add-on are gated on Hugging Face. The studio's Set up video dialog links both pages: accept the terms, create a free read token and paste it in. The download starts on its own, and the token stays on your pod instead of in Runpod's settings. Video then renders with sound and spoken dialogue, at 24 fps. Its community license is free under $10M in annual revenue, but it needs a separate license from Lightricks for products that compete with theirs, and published videos must be disclosed as AI-generated.

Can I use MiniMax H3 instead of LTX?

Yes, as an opt-in. On Runpod's deploy page, open Set overrides and set DOWNLOAD_MINIMAX_MODELS to true. Shots can then render with MiniMax H3, with sound, at 24 fps (LTX-2.5 stays the default unless a shot picks H3); Perform keeps using Wan Animate. H3's community license excludes the US, EU, UK and South Korea unless you get a license from MiniMax, so check it applies to you before turning it on.

Do I need Blender or an AI agent?

No. You can make films entirely in the browser, starting from a script. Blender is for when you want to block out the camera moves and cuts yourself, and an AI agent such as Claude Code can do all of it for you, previs included. What you need lists what each way needs.

Where can I get help?

Join the Blockbuster Studio Discord to ask questions and share what you make, or read the docs.

What's the content policy?

The studio adds no content filtering of its own. The open models are only as filtered as their publishers ship them, so you're responsible for what you make and for following each model's license.
